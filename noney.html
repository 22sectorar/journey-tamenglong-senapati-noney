Overview

Manipur's newest district, quietly unfolding

In the western highlands of Manipur, Noney unfolds quietly — valley by valley, river by river. Dense forests cloak the hillsides, while winding waterways carve through deep gorges and hidden landscapes. The air grows fresher, the pace slows, and nature takes center stage.

Known for its breathtaking terrain and rich ecological heritage, Noney is not a place of grand displays, but of quiet discovery. Rivers flow through secluded valleys, waterfalls emerge from forested slopes, and mist settles gently over the hills at dawn.

There are no towering monuments, only living stories — preserved in village life, in the traditions of the communities and in a deep connection between people and nature.

Some places are visited. Noney is experienced.

People & Living Traditions

Life in Noney is deeply rooted in community, tradition, and heritage. Predominantly inhabited by the Rongmei community alongside other Naga groups, whose customs, folklore, and traditions shape everyday life. Agriculture remains central, while bamboo crafts and oral histories preserve cultural identity across generations.

Homes in Harmony

Homes built in harmony with the landscape, strong village institutions, and a profound respect for nature reflect the spirit of Noney. Festivals, community gatherings, and ancestral traditions continue to thrive, connecting the past with the present.

The most common, reliable way to reach Noney.

Roots of Noney

Each tribe contributes to the district's rich heritage through unique traditions, colorful attire, folk dances, festivals and oral histories passed down through generations. Visitors can experience authentic tribal life — traditional handwoven textiles, sacred festivals like Gaan-Ngai, village hospitality and deep-rooted customs closely connected to nature.

History in Motion

Created in 2016 after bifurcation from Tamenglong district, Noney is one of the newest districts of Manipur, where history is still actively unfolding. Once a quiet region of remote hill villages like Longmai, Khoupum, Nungba and Haochong, Noney is now emerging as a hub of connectivity, infrastructure and cultural identity.

The construction of major projects such as the Jiribam–Imphal railway line and the world's tallest railway pier bridge near the Irang River symbolizes this transformation — from isolation to integration — while the district preserves its strong Rongmei Naga cultural roots.

Soul of the Landscape

Christianity is the predominant faith and an integral part of social and cultural life. Beyond serving as places of worship, churches function as important community institutions that strengthen unity and development across villages — known for strong traditions of choir singing, gospel festivals, and seasonal celebrations such as Palm Sunday, Christmas and Easter.

Stay & Relax

Why stay in Imphal?

Accommodation options within Noney district are limited. Tourists are advised to stay in Imphal, the capital city of Manipur, and undertake day trips to Noney. Imphal offers the widest range of hotels, resorts, homestays, restaurants, medical facilities and transport services — with taxis, buses and rental vehicles available for travel to Noney.

Tourist Advisory

  • Start early from Imphal for day trips to Noney
  • Check local travel advisories and road conditions before travelling
