Overview

Known as the Land of the Hornbill

In the western highlands of Manipur, Tamenglong reveals itself slowly — ridge by ridge, forest by forest. The air cools, the noise fades, and time loosens its grip.

This is not a destination shaped for spectacle, but for stillness. Rivers carve quietly through hidden valleys. Mist drifts across morning hills. Life here moves with the land, not against it.

There are no grand monuments — only stories. Stories held in village rhythms, in the memory of the Zeliangrong communities, and in a way of life deeply rooted in nature and season.

Some places are discovered. Tamenglong is felt.

What Awaits

Natural wonders shaped by water, forest, and time.

Barak Waterfall

SPECTACULAR CASCADE · LUSH SURROUNDINGS · PHOTOGRAPHY SPOT · ADVENTURE ACCESS

One of the hidden scenic gems of Tamenglong — a refreshing natural getaway perfect for nature exploration and a peaceful escape.

12–15 km from Tamenglong towards Khongsang road, followed by a short trek · About 60–90 min

Tharon Cave

LIMESTONE FORMATIONS · ADVENTURE EXPLORATION · HISTORICAL VALUE

Deep within the hills, Tharon Cave draws adventurers into its ancient limestone passages. The trek to Tharon Cave is a journey in itself, favoured by explorers for its raw, immersive terrain.

Zeilad Lake

RICH BIODIVERSITY · CLUSTER OF LAKES · TREKKING SPOT · ECO-TOURISM

Zeilad Lake invites stillness, its waters reflecting sky and forest alike — part of the wider Zeilad Wildlife Sanctuary.

20–22 km from Tamenglong towards Khongsang road · About 60–90 min

Buning Meadows — "The Green Paradise of Tamenglong"

VAST GREEN MEADOWS · CLOUD-KISSED HILLS · CAMPING SPOT · RICH FLORA & FAUNA

In the open stretches of Buning Meadow, the land softens into rolling green, offering some of the most serene views in the region.

45 km from Tamenglong

Orange Orchards

ORANGE FARMS · SCENIC LANDSCAPES · FARM EXPERIENCE · FRESH PRODUCE

6 km to Marrylane village · 30–60 min

Bamgaijang Spot

RIVER BEACH · SUNSET VIEWS · WATER SPORTS · PICNIC DESTINATION

Water surfing, zorbing, zip line, rafting, water rolling & motorboat rides.

12 km towards Khongsang road · 60–90 min

Alangta Waterfall

HIDDEN GEM · QUIET ESCAPE · DENSE FOREST

Getting There

From Imphal to Tamenglong, the journey is part of the destination.

By Air & Road

Begins at Bir Tikendrajit International Airport, followed by a scenic drive through forested hills. The most common, reliable way in.

161 km · 5–6 hrs (NH-37 & NH-137)
Taxi: ₹3,500–₹5,000

By Air & Helicopter

Direct helicopter landing from Imphal — a faster alternative with stunning aerial views. Limited schedule, select days only.

Fare: ₹3,500–₹4,500 per seat

By Rail & Road

Nearest railhead: Khongsang Railway Station. Currently goods trains only — check online for passenger updates.

Khongsang → Tamenglong: 43 km · ~1.5 hrs
Taxi: ₹1,500–₹2,500

People & Culture

Collectively known as the Zeliangrong community.

Rongmei

A dominant tribe, known for its role in the anti-British movement led by Haipou Jadonang and Rani Gaidinliu in the 1930s.

Liangmai

Closely connected to nature, agriculture, folklore, and traditional community life.

Zeme

A Tibeto-Burman ethnic group whose villages are spread across the district.

Inpui

One of the Naga tribes, with settlements in the Haochong area.

Emmanuel Baptist Church

One of the largest and most prominent churches in Tamenglong district, located on a hilltop in the district headquarters. The congregation began in 1972; after 12 years of construction and fundraising, the current building was inaugurated in December 2019.

Width ~71 ft · Length ~145 ft · Height ~121 ft · Seats 2,500+

Festivals

Where harvest meets heritage.

Lui-Ngai-Ni

Major seed-sowing festival on 15 February, marking the start of the agricultural season.

Gaan Ngai

The most important Zeliangrong festival, celebrated after harvest to mark the end of the agricultural year.

Chaga Ngee

A post-harvest festival of the Liangmei tribe — thanksgiving, joy, and community bonding.

Christmas

Church services, carol singing, community feasts, and the exchange of greetings.

The Orange Festival

In the hills of Tamenglong — often called the "Orange Bowl of Manipur" — the Orange Festival marks the peak of harvest season, held between December and January. It celebrates not just fruit, but land, culture, and community: traditional dances, music, tribal customs, and stalls of fresh oranges, organic produce and handcrafted goods.

Beyond festivity, it supports local farmers, strengthens the rural economy, and showcases Tamenglong's identity on a national stage.
